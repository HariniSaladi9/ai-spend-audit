# GTM.md

## Target User

The primary users are:

- Startup founders (5–50 employees)
- Engineering managers in early-stage SaaS companies
- Indie hackers using multiple AI tools (Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot)

These users all share one problem:
They use multiple AI tools but have no visibility into overspending or redundancy.

---

## User Trigger Moment

Users search or feel this pain when:

- They see rising monthly SaaS bills
- They add multiple AI tools for different tasks
- They are unsure if “Team plan” is actually needed
- Finance pressure starts increasing in startups

Typical thought:
> “Why are we paying $200+ for AI tools every month?”

---

## Where Users Come From

Primary channels:

- Twitter/X (founder + indie hacker community)
- Reddit:
  - r/startups
  - r/Entrepreneur
  - r/IndieHackers
- Hacker News (“Show HN” launch)
- AI tool communities (Cursor, Claude, OpenAI users)
- LinkedIn posts from startup founders

---

## First 100 Users Strategy (0 Budget)

1. Post audit tool on:
   - Hacker News (Show HN)
   - Indie Hackers launch post
   - Reddit startup communities

2. DM 20–30 startup founders on X:
   - Offer free AI spend audit
   - Ask them to try tool live

3. Share “before vs after savings” screenshots

4. Build viral hook:
   - “We found $200/month wasted in AI tools in 30 seconds”

---

## Unfair Advantage Channel

Credex has access to discounted AI credits.

So the distribution advantage is:
- Users discover overspending
- Credex can directly offer cheaper AI infrastructure

This turns the tool into:
> a lead-generation engine, not just a utility

---

## Week 1 Success Metrics

If the product works:

- 100–300 users
- 30–50 audits completed
- 10–20% email conversion rate
- 5–10 high-savings leads (> $100/month saved)

---

## Why This Product Will Spread

Because it is:

- Instant (no login required)
- Financially emotional (savings shown immediately)
- Shareable (people post savings screenshots)
- Useful even once (no repeat friction)

---

## Positioning

This is not a dashboard.

It is:
> “Mint for AI tool spending”
